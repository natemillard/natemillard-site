## The problem

Most partnerships fail politely. Two organizations meet, agree that working together would be valuable, produce a memorandum, take a photograph, and then discover over the following year that neither has any capacity allocated to the thing they agreed on.

Nobody lied. Both meant it. What was missing was the specific overlap: not shared values, which every organization has, but the particular piece of work where their priorities and ours genuinely need each other.

Agreement is cheap and it feels like progress. That is what makes it dangerous.

> Two organizations can agree completely about a problem and still have no overlapping capacity to do anything about it.

<Callout label="The shift">
From asking whether we agree to asking what each of us is actually going to spend. Alignment is not a state of mind. It is two organizations putting real time, people or money against the same specific thing, for reasons that make sense from both sides.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Two columns and what sits between them. It takes fifteen minutes and it is uncomfortable in a useful way.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"What they need"}>
Not what they say in public. What is on their board agenda, what their funder is asking about, what would make their year easier. If you cannot fill this column, you do not know them well enough to partner with them yet.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"What we need"}>
Written just as plainly. Being vague about our own interest reads as either naive or evasive, and partners notice both.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"The overlap"}>
The specific work that serves both. Specific is the word. "Community resilience" is not an overlap, it is a category. "You need to reach families you are not reaching and we need a trusted venue" is an overlap.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"The honest gap"}>
What each side wants that the other genuinely cannot help with. Naming this is what makes the overlap believable. A partnership where everything aligns has not been examined.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

**The hardest question, asked out loud:** what does this partner actually get from us? If the room goes quiet, that silence is the finding.

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Fill both columns"} time={"15 minutes, alone"} finding={"The first column is usually much harder to fill than the second. That gap is the state of the relationship."}>
Pick one real partner. Write what they need and what we need, in plain terms, side by side.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Ask the benefit question"} time={"2 minutes, honestly"} finding={"If the answer is our brand or our reach, check whether they would agree. Often they would not."}>
What does this partner get from us that they could not get elsewhere?
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Find the specific thing"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"Most stated alignments survive one round of this and not two."}>
Take your overlap and make it more specific twice. Then again. Stop when it names an actual piece of work in an actual place.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Name the gap out loud"} time={"One conversation"} finding={"It feels like it will damage the relationship. It reliably does the opposite, because it makes everything else you said credible."}>
Tell a partner one thing they want that we cannot help with.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
