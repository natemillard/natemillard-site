## The problem

Chapters rarely choose where to work by weighing evidence. They choose by proximity: the neighborhood a volunteer lives in, the partner who returned a call, the school that said yes last year, the place somebody drove past and felt something about.

None of that is bad faith. All of it is how the work has always been allocated, and it is why two neighborhoods with the same risk profile can get very different amounts of attention for reasons nobody could defend out loud.

The awkward part is that we usually have the data. It sits in a dashboard nobody opens because the decision has already been made by the time anyone thinks to look.

> The problem is almost never a shortage of numbers. It is that the decision happens before anybody looks at them.

**The word is informed, not driven.** Data supports judgment, it does not replace it. A dashboard can tell you participation is low. It cannot tell you the meetings are at 6pm in a neighborhood where most people work evenings. That takes asking someone.

## The one thing to know

Two kinds of evidence and one question. Most decisions use one kind and skip the question entirely.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"Quantitative"}>
Fire incident rates, census demographics, social vulnerability and risk indices, program participation numbers. Good at telling you where and how much. Silent on why.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Qualitative"}>
What residents say, what partners have seen, what happened last time, what people are worried about. Good at telling you why and what would actually work. Easy to dismiss as anecdote.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"The question"}>
What would I have to see to change my mind? Ask it before you look at anything. If there is no answer, you are not analyzing, you are gathering support for a decision you already made.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

**Neither kind wins.** A number without a story misreads the situation, and a story without a number misjudges the scale.

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Write down the answer first"} time={"2 minutes, before your next look at data"} finding={"You will find out whether you are analyzing or confirming, which is worth knowing."}>
Before you open the dashboard, write down what you expect it to say and what would surprise you.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Find one number you do not have"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"Often it does and nobody looked. Sometimes it does not, which is also a finding worth acting on."}>
Take a decision you made this year and ask what evidence would have made it better. Then find out whether that evidence exists.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Ask why the number is what it is"} time={"One conversation"} finding={"The answer is usually operational and fixable, and it is never in the dashboard."}>
Pick a low participation number and ask somebody who lives there why they think it is low.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Close one loop"} time={"15 minutes"} finding={"This is the step everybody skips. A cycle that never closes is not learning, it is just reporting."}>
Take something you changed based on evidence and check whether it worked. Then tell somebody the answer.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
