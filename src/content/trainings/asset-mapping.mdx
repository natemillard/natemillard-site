## The problem

Almost every plan starts with a need. A gap analysis, a risk index, a list of what is missing. It is a reasonable place to start and it produces a reliable result: a community described entirely by its problems, and a plan that only we can deliver.

Meanwhile the barber who knows everyone, the church that already runs a food line, the retired nurse three doors down, and the group chat that mobilized in four hours during the last outage do not appear anywhere in the assessment.

They are the reason anything survives after we leave.

> Every community you work in is already responding to its own problems. The question is whether your plan knows that.

<Callout label="The shift">
From assessing a community to inventorying it. Needs assessment asks what is wrong here. Asset mapping asks what is already working, who already has standing, and what would keep working if we never came back.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Four categories. Most people find the first one and stop, which is exactly the problem.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"Individuals"}>
The skills, knowledge and capacity of people who live there. The retired teacher, the person who speaks three languages, the one everybody calls when something goes wrong. Rarely on any list, and usually the most important column.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Associations"}>
Informal and voluntary groups. Block clubs, faith groups, sports leagues, mutual aid networks, the group chat. No budget, no letterhead, real reach.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Institutions"}>
Schools, clinics, libraries, businesses, government offices. The easiest to find, which is why maps that only have this column look complete and are not.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Physical and economic"}>
Land, buildings, transport, spaces people gather, money that circulates locally. Where things happen matters as much as who makes them happen.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

<Callout label="The check" tone="clay">
If your map is mostly institutions, you have made a directory, not a map.
</Callout>

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Fill the four corners"} time={"15 minutes, alone"} finding={"One box will be much thinner than the others. That is the finding, and it is usually Individuals or Associations."}>
Take one community you work in. Draw four boxes and fill each with as many real, specific, named things as you can. Not categories, names.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Name five people with no title"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"If you cannot get to five, that is not a fact about the community. It is a fact about how you have been engaging with it."}>
List five people in that community with real influence who hold no formal position.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Ask the trust question"} time={"One conversation"} finding={"The answer is rarely the person on our contact list, and it is almost always worth the conversation."}>
Ask somebody who lives there: when something goes wrong here, who do people actually go to?
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Check your map against a resident"} time={"20 minutes"} finding={"This is the whole skill in miniature. Everything before it is a hypothesis."}>
Show your four boxes to one person who lives there and ask what is missing or wrong.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
