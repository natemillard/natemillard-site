## The problem

A chapter starts something good. Staff convene the meetings, write the agendas, make the follow-up calls, hold the relationships and keep the momentum. It works, and it works because of them.

Two years later the same staff are still doing all of it, the program cannot survive a personnel change, and everyone describes it as community-led because the community shows up.

Nothing went wrong. That is what makes it hard. The early leadership was necessary, and the same behavior became the ceiling.

> Participation is not ownership. The test is not who attends. It is who decides, and who would carry it if we left.

<Callout label="The shift">
From asking when do we hand this over to asking how we build ownership from the start. Transfer at the end is a rescue. Ownership is a series of small shifts made while things are going well, which is exactly when nobody feels the need to make them.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Four questions that tell you who actually holds a program. Answer them about something you run.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"Who sets the agenda?"}>
Not who chairs the meeting. Who decides what is on it. If it is us, the community is attending our program, however warmly.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Who holds the relationships?"}>
If every partner relationship runs through one staff member, the program is one resignation from ending, and everyone can see that except us.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Who does the work between meetings?"}>
The follow-up calls, the reminders, the chasing. This is where ownership actually lives, and it is the least visible part.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"What happens if we leave tomorrow?"}>
Not whether it would be harder. Whether it would continue at all. The honest answer to this question is the real maturity level of the program.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

<Callout label="The uncomfortable finding" tone="clay">
Most people place their program a level higher than the evidence supports. The four questions are designed to make that visible without anyone having to say it.
</Callout>

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"The ownership map"} time={"15 minutes, alone"} finding={"Count the \"only we can\" column. Then ask, for each one, whether that is a capability, a credential, a liability, or just a habit."}>
List the ten tasks that keep one program running. Beside each write who does it, and mark whether only we can do it, someone else could, or someone else already does.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"If we left tomorrow"} time={"5 minutes"} finding={"Most people can answer this in one sentence and most have never written it down. Writing it changes what you do next week."}>
Write down honestly what would happen to one program if your chapter stepped away next month.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Hand over one thing"} time={"One month"} finding={"The second half is the exercise. The first half is easy."}>
Pick the smallest real thing that somebody else could do and give it away. Then do not take it back when it is done differently.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Ask what it would take"} time={"One conversation"} finding={"People routinely say yes to more than we assume, and their answer is almost never what we would have guessed."}>
Ask a partner directly what they would need in order to run part of this themselves.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
