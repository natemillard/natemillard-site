## The problem

Add up the hours your chapter spends in meetings this month, multiply by the people in them, and you have the single biggest recurring investment the chapter makes. Now ask who designed any of them.

A recurring invitation gets created for a good reason. The reason ends. The invitation does not. Two years later fourteen people attend something nobody can name the purpose of, and the person who could cancel it assumes somebody else needs it.

The cost is not the hour. It is that the real conversation then happens in twos and threes afterward, which is slower and excludes whoever was not in the corridor.

> If you cannot say what a meeting is for in one sentence, the meeting is not the problem. The absence of that sentence is.

<Callout label="The shift">
From running meetings to designing them. A meeting is a leadership tool with a job to do, and like any tool it can be the wrong one. Some of what you currently meet about should be an email, and some of what you currently email about needs a room.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Meetings fail in three places, and they are three different failures with three different fixes.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"Before"}>
No stated outcome, the wrong people invited, and an agenda that is a list of topics rather than a sequence of decisions. Most of what goes wrong during a meeting was determined before it started.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"During"}>
Drift, one voice dominating, discussion that never converts into a decision, and the quiet person who had the answer and was never asked.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"After"}>
Notes that record what was said rather than what was decided. Actions with no owner and no date. Then the slow discovery three weeks later that nothing happened.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

**The one change worth making first:** write the outcome as a sentence before you write the agenda. “By the end we will have decided X.” If you cannot finish that sentence, cancel it or make it an email.

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Meeting or email?"} time={"5 minutes"} finding={"You will find at least one that has been a broadcast for a year. Cancelling it buys back more time than any facilitation technique on this page."}>
Take your recurring meetings. For each one, ask whether it needs a conversation or just needs information moved.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Write the outcome sentence"} time={"2 minutes, next meeting you call"} finding={"If it takes more than two minutes to write, that is the finding. The meeting was not ready."}>
Before the agenda, write: by the end of this meeting we will have ______.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Name the decider"} time={"1 minute per agenda item"} finding={"Most drift is caused by items where nobody knows whether the group is advising or deciding."}>
For each item, write who decides. Not who is interested, who decides.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Rewrite one set of notes"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"Compare the length. What is left is what the meeting actually produced."}>
Take your last meeting notes and rewrite them as decisions and actions only, each with a name and a date.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
