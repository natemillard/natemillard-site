## The problem

A project stalls and everybody has a theory. Not enough time, the wrong season, the partner went quiet. The real reason is usually simpler and duller: somebody with the power to stop it was never in the conversation, or somebody who cared deeply was never asked.

It is rarely deliberate. We engage the people we already know, who are the people who answer, who are the people we already know. The map draws itself around our own contact list.

Then something goes wrong and a name comes up that nobody in the room had heard.

> The people who can stop your work and the people who care most about it are usually two different groups, and neither is your contact list.

<Callout label="The shift">
From listing who is involved to reading who holds what. Influence and interest are separate things, and the four combinations need four completely different responses.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Two questions produce four groups. How much influence do they have over whether this happens, and how much do they actually care?

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"High / High"}>
High influence, high interest. Work closely with them, involve them in decisions, and do not surprise them. This group is small and it is where your time goes.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"High / Low"}>
High influence, low interest. They can stop you and are not paying attention. Keep them satisfied and informed. Most projects that fail late fail here, because nobody kept this box warm.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Low / High"}>
Low influence, high interest. They care most and hold the least formal power, which usually means residents. Keep them genuinely involved, and be honest that you are not handing them a veto you cannot give.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Low / Low"}>
Low influence, low interest. Monitor. Some of these move, and the ones that move tend to move fast.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

<Callout label="Then the harder question" tone="clay">
Who is missing from all four boxes? The people affected by this who have no route into any of them are the ones a map is actually for.
</Callout>

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Build the board"} time={"15 minutes, alone"} finding={"The sorting argument you have with yourself is the exercise. Where something goes matters less than noticing you were not sure."}>
Take one live piece of work. Write every person and organization with a stake on separate sticky notes, then place them in the four boxes.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Find the missing landmark"} time={"5 minutes"} finding={"There is always at least one. Often it is the group the work is supposedly for."}>
Look at your four boxes and ask who is affected by this and appears nowhere.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Check the trust axis"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"Influence tells you who matters. Trust tells you what you can actually ask of them this month."}>
Go through your high-influence names and mark each one: do they trust us, are they neutral, or have we let them down before?
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Have one conversation"} time={"30 minutes"} finding={"Almost everyone can name that person immediately, and almost nobody has made the call."}>
Pick the highest-influence person you have not spoken to and go speak to them. No agenda, just understanding what they need.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
