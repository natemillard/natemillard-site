## The problem

A chapter has capacity for about seven things and is currently doing fourteen. Nobody decided that. It happened one reasonable yes at a time. A project got added. An initiative got extended. A committee got formed. A partner asked and the answer was of course.

Nothing was ever removed.

What follows is not that anything visibly fails. It is that everything gets done at about seventy percent, staff are tired in a way that shows up in no report, and nobody can say which of the fourteen things they would protect if something had to give.

> A priority that receives insufficient attention is not a priority. It is an aspiration.

<Callout label="The shift">
Prioritization is not deciding what matters. Many things matter. It is deciding where limited staff time, volunteer capacity, funding and attention can do the most. Avoiding that decision does not remove the tradeoff. It makes it invisible, and it lets it get made by whoever asked most recently or most loudly.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Three tiers. It takes ten minutes to apply to your own list, and the argument it starts is the useful part.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"Must"}>
Mission-critical, required, time-sensitive, or creates real risk if it does not happen. Disaster response obligations. Safety responsibilities. Required reporting. Commitments a partner is relying on.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Should"}>
Important, advances your goals, and can be adjusted, sequenced or delayed if capacity is tight. Volunteer development. Preparedness outreach. New partnership cultivation. Process improvement.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"May"}>
Valuable if resources allow, and postponable without real consequence. Optional enhancements. New pilot ideas. Additional events. Visibility opportunities not tied to a need.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

<Callout label="The trap" tone="clay">
Teams put almost everything in Must. When everything is a Must, the framework has told you nothing. That is the moment the real conversation starts.
</Callout>

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"The honest time audit"} time={"10 minutes, once"} finding={"They will add up to well over a hundred percent. That is the finding, and it is why the list has never felt manageable."}>
List the six to ten things you are responsible for. Beside each, write roughly what share of your time it actually took last month. Not what it should have taken.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Sort it, then count your musts"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"If more than about a third landed in Must, take the two you are least sure of and defend each one to a colleague against the definition above. Roughly half do not survive."}>
Put every item into Must, Should or May. Then count the Musts.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"The two questions"} time={"30 seconds, any time"} finding={"The gap between those two answers places the item. Urgent things create pressure. Important things create impact. They are not the same axis."}>
For anything you are unsure about, ask: what happens if this does not happen today? And what happens if this does not happen six months from now?
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Name one displacement"} time={"2 minutes, next time you say yes"} finding={"A decision presented as costless is not believed, and the delayed work stays quietly expected. This one sentence is most of what people mean by transparency."}>
When you agree to something new, say out loud what it pushed back, and by when.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
