## The problem

A community has a housing problem, a transport problem and a preparedness problem, and they are the same problem. Thirteen organizations are working on it. Each has a strategy, a funder, a set of outcomes, and a genuine commitment.

They meet quarterly, share updates, agree that collaboration is important, and go back to delivering their own thing measured in their own way. Everybody is working hard. Nobody is working together.

The gap is not goodwill. It is that no one owns the shared agenda, so every organization optimizes for the only thing it is actually accountable for.

> Coordination is organizations telling each other what they are doing. A shared agenda is organizations changing what they do.

<Callout label="The shift">
From partnership as a relationship to partnership as an operating structure. Getting several organizations to genuinely move together requires a small number of unglamorous conditions, and the whole thing fails quietly when any one of them is missing.
</Callout>

## The one thing to know

Five conditions. They come from the collective impact literature and they hold up in practice, mostly by predicting exactly how these efforts fall apart.

<FrameworkGrid>

<FrameworkItem title={"A common agenda"}>
One shared definition of the problem and what change would look like. Not a list of everyone's priorities stapled together, which is the usual outcome of asking politely.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Shared measurement"}>
Everyone tracking a small number of the same things, the same way. This is the condition that gets dropped first and it is the one that makes the others real.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Mutually reinforcing activities"}>
Each organization doing what it is best at, deliberately sequenced so the pieces add up. Not everyone doing a bit of everything.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Continuous communication"}>
Frequent enough to build trust between people who are competing for the same funding. Quarterly is not frequent enough, and everyone knows it.
</FrameworkItem>

<FrameworkItem title={"Backbone support"}>
Somebody whose actual job is holding the thing together. Without it the work falls to whoever has the least capacity to refuse, and then it stops.
</FrameworkItem>

</FrameworkGrid>

<Callout label="The honest question for us" tone="clay">
Is the Red Cross the backbone here, a contributing partner, or the wrong organization for this table? All three are legitimate answers and only one of them is usually true.
</Callout>

## Four things to try this week

Pick one. Not four.

<PracticeGrid>

<Practice title={"Write the agenda in one sentence"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"If you cannot, that is the state of the common agenda, and it explains most of what is frustrating about those meetings."}>
Take a coalition you sit in. Write what it is trying to achieve in one sentence, without using the word and.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Check the measurement"} time={"5 minutes"} finding={"If nobody shares a metric, the group is a forum. That is not a criticism, but it should be named, because forums and partnerships need different things."}>
List what each organization in that group reports on.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Find your actual contribution"} time={"10 minutes"} finding={"The second half is what makes the first half credible to the other twelve organizations."}>
Write what your organization is genuinely best at in this effort, and what you should stop doing because somebody else does it better.
</Practice>

<Practice title={"Name the backbone"} time={"2 minutes"} finding={"If the answer is nobody, or it is somebody doing it on top of a full role, you have found the reason for the drift."}>
Ask who holds this together, and whether it is anybody's actual job.
</Practice>

</PracticeGrid>
