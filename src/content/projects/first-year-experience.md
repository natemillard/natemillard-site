First-year programmes usually fail in the same place: they treat belonging as
an outcome to be produced rather than a condition students either find or
don't. Most of what worked came from the peer mentors, who were closer to the
experience than any of us and were mostly under-trained for what we were
asking of them.

So a large share of the effort went into developing the mentors themselves —
structured coaching, reflection, and real leadership practice, on the theory
that a well-supported nineteen-year-old is more use to a frightened
eighteen-year-old than any programme design.

I taught into these courses as well, on the social construction of identity
and how group participation shapes it, which is the same question I now ask
about communities rather than classrooms.
