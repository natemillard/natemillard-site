The design problem in a programme like this is that participants arrive from
contexts far more varied than any curriculum can address. A syllabus written
for all of them addresses none of them.

So the structure leaned on experience and reflection rather than content
delivery — field visits chosen to disagree with each other, and time built in
for participants to work out what applied at home and what did not. The
learning tours were deliberately different from one another: a state capital,
a post-industrial city, a federal centre. Three incompatible models of the
same idea is a better teacher than one coherent one.

The applied projects were the point. Everything before them was preparation.
