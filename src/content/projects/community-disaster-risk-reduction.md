Twenty-six counties is a large enough area that no single organisation can
serve it directly, which turns out to be the useful constraint. The work was
less about delivering programmes than about putting resources and decisions
closer to the people who would be using them.

That meant grant processes designed so that small community organisations
could actually navigate them, infrastructure chosen with the groups who would
operate it, and a standing regional network rather than a series of one-off
convenings.

The measure I cared about was not how much we distributed but how much kept
working without us — whether a resilience hub still opened, whether a partner
went on to win funding on their own.
