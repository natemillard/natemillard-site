Universities are good at assessing whether students know something and poor at
assessing whether it changed how they live. For sustainability education that
gap is the whole question: knowledge alone does not produce the change the
knowledge implies.

The pathway itself was interdisciplinary by design, pulling perspectives from
eighteen disciplines rather than housing sustainability in one department. The
harder half of the work was the assessment instrument — building something
that could look at attitudes and behaviours alongside knowledge, and at how
those three intersect.

The guiding question came from Dewey: to understand something is to see it in
its relations to other things, to note how it operates, what follows from it.
The question for us was where to look for evidence of that, and whether it
survived graduation.
