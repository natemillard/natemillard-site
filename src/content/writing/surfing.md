Somewhere in transportation, somebody dropped my surfboard,
The puncture broke through the layers of glass and crushed into the foam core.
I decided to repair it myself. I cut the broken fiberglass away, I scraped the damaged foam out, sanded around the wound, then filled it with a epoxy filler. Once dry, I sanded for hours until it was even and smooth where the gouges once were. After that, I cut the fiberglass cloth and dipped into the epoxy for a glass coating. After the second coat dried, I sanded and sanded. The smell of surf wax in wafting with the onshore winds, the fine, white dust embedded into the pores and calluses of my hands. Once it was done, I waited for a swell. After three days of nothing but cold wind, and no swell, I decided to paddle out anyway. I carried my repaired surfboard with me, climbed over the parked railcars along the track next to highway one. I stepped onto the sand, the sun shifting colors in the parabolic refraction of our atmosphere. I pulled the too dry wetsuit over my skin, tucked my clothes into my backpack and left it along the sastrugi dunes, then ran to the water’s edge. The waves were small, choppy, erratic, and incongruent. The tide was low and turning. The cold water seeped into my left leg, the leg without the leash sealing the water out. The first dive into the water was a homecoming. Nobody else was out there.
