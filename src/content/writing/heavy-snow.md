The Incense Cedar and Doug Fir trees dip into the street under the snow load; they lean against snow-laden power lines. It is a storm for blackouts in the high country. The Sierra-cement drops like depression on the mind. When it becomes obvious we can’t make it into the wilderness for the night, we stop, and let the dog out of Erin’s Subaru Forester to run alongside in the snow for a few minutes. I park, get out, and chase him in the tracks of a four-wheel drive truck while Erin waits. We come off the mountain and turn the car to go back to Erin’s parent’s house—a safe place in a storm. Highway 5 splits the great valley of California, and each off-ramp signals a different place to go, because nobody stays on this road forever. In order to make the highway go faster and smoother, the small-town roads and slow-moving railcars pass over the straight paved road on spanning cement bridges. As we drive in the loud puncturing raindrops on the windshield, I wait for each bridge and overpass. For just a fraction of a second everything appears to be quiet—quiet like the way snow is supposed to fall in the forest.
